Behind the scenes

How we measure, and how to read a score

ida·llm is a faculty-facing benchmark. Models are scored against published programme learning outcomes, not trivia quizzes or our taste.

What you are looking at

This site is evidence, not advice. A high score with low confidence is not yet a verdict. Home shows majors. View All Classes opens the ranking table: models down the left, classes across the top, scores out of 100.

How a number is made

  • A class score such as MIS 200 is a sample-weighted mean across work that has actually been scored. Unmeasured work is left out, never counted as zero.
  • Confidence is about the measurement — how it was marked, over how many attempts, and whether anything failed — never about the model.
  • Not measured yet means nobody has run the test. That is different from having tested it and found it wanting.
  • A failed run is excluded from a score. An outage must not make a model look incompetent.
  • The yardstick is yours: published programme outcomes.

One outcome, three jobs

The same published SLO can be asked three ways. We keep them separate so a model that writes well is not confused with a model that tutors well.

BScBA General Business (core) · SLO2 · Quantitative Literacy · as published

“Students analyze, solve, and communicate effectively regarding quantitative business problems using appropriate software, data sources, and tools.”

Can the model itself produce work that would satisfy the outcome, given the same prompt a student is given? The model is the candidate.

Not measured yet 8 tasks ask this question, but no model has a finished, scored run against them yet.

Same outcome · tutoring lens

Does the model improve a student's own work against the outcome without doing it for them — feedback, critique, a next question? The model is a tutor and the student is the candidate.

Not measured yet The curriculum defines this question, but no task has been written against it yet.

Same outcome · staff lens

Can the model do the instructor's side of the outcome — author an item, apply a marking key, grade against a rubric? The model is staff.

What we do not do

We do not average unmeasured work in as zero. We do not hide a refusal behind a smooth recommendation. If the sample is too thin, Learn more and the recommendation flow both say so.

A recommendation for my class

A few questions, then an answer that says what it rests on.

Question 1 of 3

Are you a teacher, a researcher, or both?

Teaching tasks and scholarship tasks are benchmarked separately. Pick one to continue.

What subject(s) do you teach?

Choose as many as apply. You will get a recommendation for each one.

Is this for your own work, or for your students to use?

A tool you use yourself and a tool you put in front of students need different guardrails. Pick one to continue.

What do you want help with?

Choose as many as apply. The recommendation will speak to each area.

How to read this
  1. No AI knowledge needed. Questions are about teaching or research, not technology. “I’m not sure” is a real option.
  2. About two minutes, one question at a time. You will not see every question — later ones depend on earlier answers.
  3. You may get a refusal, and that is the product. If we have not measured enough, we say what is missing instead of guessing.
